The main goal of the *Potentate’s Guide to the Environs* (nicknamed PGE4) is to create an alternate Tamriel that is more vibrant and strange a la the original *Pocket Guide to the Empire*. It is set 200 years after the events of *The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim* in a timeline where a continent-wide plague and a Second Great War destroyed most of the old political orders. New cultures, factions, nations, and religions are rising from the ashes. Our aim as writers and artists is to describe these cultures, factions, nations, and religions through the lens of a biased travel guide. Below are some of the central design pillars the team agreed upon in the early days of the project:

**Central Design Pillars:**

1. **The PGE4 is a collaborative project.** We operate on the “Yes, and…” principle of trying to build on previous work rather than override it entirely. When conflicts arise we try to discuss it diplomatically on the project’s Discord server. Remember, we’re here to have fun!
2. **Old national identities are falling apart.** We are not interested in the “Redguards come from the Redguard land of Hammerfall, Bosmer come from the Bosmer land of Valenwood” style of worldbuilding that permeates so much of mainstream fantasy and even much of The Elder Scrolls. The lines around ethnic and national identity are as blurry in this version of Tamriel as they are in our universe. Ethnonationalism can be depicted as a political ideology within the world, but it is not the default setting as it is in so much fantasy.
3. **Tamriel is in a multi-polar Cold War.** There are a few Big Powers (the [[Second Potentate]], the [[Freehold Republic]], the [[Yokedate]], [[Argonia]], etc.), but no one wants to risk another Great War by taking a direct shot right now. So instead they fund proxy conflicts, get in trade wars, enact games of subterfuge, encourage piracy, and so on.
4. **There are no clear “good guys” or “bad guys” on the international stage.** One of the early hopes for the project was that it could inspire other future fanfiction, TTRPG campaigns, or what-have-you. Therefore, none of the polities are meant to be wholly “good” or “evil,” but they could be pushed in either direction in the future. This does not mean we should feel obligated to artificially stack each faction with a list of “good” and “bad” qualities until they are all perfectly balanced, merely that we should try to avoid turning any of these fictional societies into utopias or dystopias.
5. **Each nation is in a state of flux.** As mentioned before, as of the writing of the Potentate’s Guide, Tamriel is finally fully recovered from a Plague/War combination that killed anywhere from a third to half of the continent’s population. The world is changing, socially, spiritually, and even technologically through a soul-powered industrial revolution. This makes the setting something like Tamriel’s equivalent of Europe’s Age of Exploration. Arcano-capitalism is en vogue, and so is colonialism and military adventurism.
6. **We are not writing for a video game.** As such, we do not need to concern ourselves with game limitations. Tamriel is enormous (as large as the Roman Empire in 177 CE if we believe *Daggerfall*’s sense of scale), and there are likely countless towns, cities, and people we didn’t see on-screen in the games. Most cities likely were larger than we saw in-game, and probably have more than just one temple or shrine. Think about all the interesting things that might exist in an area. With things like magic, we do not have to worry about "game balance” anymore. “Monsters” (Centaurs, Goblins, Minotaurs, etc.) should be treated as people and have varying levels of integration and conflict with the other other cultures of Tamriel.
7. **Realistic conflict is Cool.** Religious conflict does not happen “because people can’t agree on which gods to worship”. Religion is often tied to sociocultural identities, and it leads to a more realistic (and interesting!) conflict if that angle is explored. Similarly, nations merely attacking each other to “reclaim the lost glories” is silly and cliche. Finding social, cultural, and economic angles to a conflict can lead to better storytelling.
8. **The Potentate’s Guide is biased.** It is edited by a government-funded group called the [[Cheydinhal Geographical Society]] at the behest of Potentate [[Hlaalu Helseth]] Ra’athim (yes, the Helseth from *Daggerfall* and *Morrowind*). It is, then, essentially state-sponsored media for a highly neoliberal technocratic oligarchy. Though they claim to have transcended the Septim-era imperialism of their past, they are blind to their own cultural imperialism. The only commentary is written by an Orc sailor named [[Yzmul gra-Maluk]], who grew up in Cheydinhal and has a cynical view of the Potentate, religion, and anything “elitist” in general; with a conversely romantic view of “populist” groups like the Baandari pirates and the Orc whale-sailors of Orsinium.
9. **Consider the reader.** Dividing up the chapter into sections for improved readability is very helpful (consider "geography/history/points of interest" a rough template). Furthermore, don't get too bogged down recapping ancient history and lore from TES. The Potentate’s Guide is a travel guide focused on tourism and trade, and the real-life reader can always check a wiki if they want to know more.
