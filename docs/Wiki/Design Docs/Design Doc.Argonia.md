**Background:**
[[Argonia]] is now united under a confederation of tribes led by a priest-king with the common goal of never again being conquered by an outside force. The loss of Lilmoth to the Sload (“New Thras”) has seemingly only confirmed the need for this common goal. The three main factions, however, are divided into what Argonia should do next: the “Blood” faction want to return to Argonia’s ancient past of empire-building and human sacrifice, the “Stone” faction advocate modernism and trade with outsiders, and the majority “Swamp” faction prefer isolationism.

Argonia is influenced by Black Marsh’s depiction in *Pocket Guide to the Empire, Third Edition*, Argonian lore from *Elder Scrolls Online*, the Greg Keyes *Elder Scrolls* novels, and pre-colonial nations of the Amazons.

**Society and Culture:**
Argonia is united by a “King/Queen of Argonia”, but this figure’s role is largely spiritual and symbolic. Each region of Argonia is actually governed by tribal councils, which occasionally involve hereditary rule, and usually involve the religious involvement of “tree-minders”. People of mannish and merish descent are treated better depending on A) how assimilated into Argonian culture they are and B) whether their ancestors sided with the Argonians when they rebelled against the Empire or not.

Religiously, Sithis worship is predominant, though in various diverse forms, and a surging faith in the “Brotherhood of Seth(iete)” has been on the rise. Culturally, there is also some diversity in the “Kothringi revivalist” tradition along western parts of Argonia, and a bit of tension due to the large number of Dunmer living in northeastern Argonia.

**Relationships with Others:**
Argonia has the most tension with the Potentate and Resdayn, due to Argonia’s desire to control the Topal Bay and the Thirr River. Argonia also does not enjoy having the necromantic raiders of New Thras on their southern borders, or the piratical raiders of the Baandari Coast.

**Main Themes:**
The Argonia chapter focuses on the Argonians’ attempt at nation-building in the wake of retreating imperialism and colonialism, with the Argonians themselves finding temptations to engage in their own forms of imperialism.
