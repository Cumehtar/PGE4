**Background**:
The [[Second Potentate]] is the eastern Nibenese remnant of the old Empire. The last Mede Emperor, [[Albertius Mede]], in a last-ditch effort to win populist support for his regime, claimed to have found a clause in Versidue-Shaie’s Guilds’ Act allowing him to empower the guilds and grant guildmasters seats on the Elder Council. He was assassinated anyway, civil war ensued, and when the dust settled the Elder Council had elected a new “Potentate" in a fit of Akaviri revivalism.

Meanwhile, [[Hlaalu Helseth]] had been slowly rising through the ranks from [[Cheydinhal]], first as an advisor to Count Farwil Indarys from the Hlaalu, later as Count Cheydinhal, before eventually becoming the Potentate himself. Helseth cleverly plays the nobles, industrialists, and labor guilds against each other, being equally loved and hated by all factions. In the meantime he has built a powerful secret police for himself using rogue agents of the [[Morag Tong]] and the [[Dark Brotherhood]]. When Helseth finally dies, which faction wins out?

The Second Potentate is a bit of a fusion between Imperial China, the Byzantine Empire, and an industrialized early-modern society influenced by Nibenay’s depiction in the original *Pocket Guide to the Empire* as well as New Crobuzon from China Miéville’s Bas-Lag trilogy. It places heavy emphasis on the importance of bureaucracy and technocracy.

**Society and Culture:**
As noted, the Potentate is divided between the nobles and the magic-industrialists, and the labor guilds. The civil service placement exams, which anyone can enter and theoretically pass with proper education, are meant to erode some of the class divide. In practice, those who pass the exams usually come from families of status (like in Imperial China). There is also some rivalry between the Synod, a guild of mages who focus on domestic production, and the EEC, who love outsourcing when profitable.

Nibenay is just as diverse as it was described in the original Pocket Guide to the Empire, with the theological upheavals following the collapse of the Empire seeing a revival of the “land of a thousand cults”. The result is three main religious strains that don’t necessarily contradict each other: the monistic Oneness tradition of the Neo-Alessian cult, the pantheistic tradition of the Maran cult with its array of Saints who guide others to Enlightenment, and the Arkayn tradition of ancestor worship influenced by Dunmeri rites. Most citizens of the Potentate probably engage in practices of all three, with only the most dedicated priests caring to differentiate the theology at work.

**Relationships with Others:**
The Potentate seems to have the best relationship with the [[Archdiocese]]; who it sees as a relatively stable neighbor and reliable trade partner, [[Greater Wrothgaria and Karth]]; who it similarly sees as stable and worthy of trading with, and the [[Iliac League]], who it would like to have a stronger trade relationship with. Its opinion of the [[Sapiarchy of Alinor]] is cool to neutral. Currently, the Potentate is fixated on the southern trade routes, bringing them in conflict with [[Pelletine]] and [[Argonia]]. There is also cultural tension with [[Resdayn]] due to ancestral burial rites; the Hlaalu would like their tombs in Narsis and the Dres would like their tombs in Kragenmoor. They have allied with [[Anequina]] in the past to repel Baandari pirates, but certain factions in the Potentate would also like to see the return of the lucrative port at [[Leyawiin]] to Nibenese control. They respect the [[Freehold Republic]]’s mercantilism, but see them as a rival in trade and colonial pursuits. The [[Yokedate]]’s militancy in both religious and naval affairs disturbs them.

**Main Themes**:
The overarching narrative of the Potentate is that of growing oligarchic corruption and stagnation, but also a nascent push for real democracy and labor rights. The [[East Empire Company]] (here reimagined as a monopolistic megacorp, befitting their portrayal in TESIII) and Potentate Helseth are the main sponsors of the Guide, making much of the in-universe writing something of a propaganda piece for them and their highly elitist worldview. Still, there is much to admire in the Potentate, as it is genuinely one of the most culturally, religiously, and ethnically diverse regions in the known world. Especially on the local level, various guilds are able to enact change and gain power for themselves.

Another major theme is technocracy and techno-fetishism. Many in the Potentate are rushing to embrace soul-industry and large-scale arcology projects (see [[Port Katariah]]), perhaps to their own societal detriment. The rule-by-bureacracy can also be explored in other ways, such as the divide between [[Cropsford]]’s style of agriculture (said to be more farmer-led) and Cheydinhal’s style of agriculture (said to be more political).
