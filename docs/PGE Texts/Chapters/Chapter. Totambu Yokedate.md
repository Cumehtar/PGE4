The westernmost state of the continent, the region which is still, disgracefully, often referred to by the Septim moniker ‘Hammerfell’, is one of the most wondrous, but also most dangerous travel destinations for a visitor. Not least because unlike the wind-swept steppes or pirate-infested waters one would cross on the way there, the danger is not in the obvious, physical violence. No, the biggest danger of that land is navigating its customs and rites, for however natural and self-evident they may seem to the inhabitants, it is impossible for a foreigner not to treat them as exotic, rigid rules.

Even naming it a single state is a trap, for it is not one state, but three, whose borders almost, but not fully, overlap. A brief historical excursion is necessary at this point, for the reader to understand the shape and the outline of the current situation.

The short period after the First Great War and the secession of the Hammerfell province was a time of great unity. Brought together by the hatred toward both the aggressive Thalmor and the spineless Mede Empire, the whole diversity of the Hammerfell society welcomed the re-establishment of the ancient title of [[Elden Yokeda]] for his most faithful general by the High King of the time. The urban Redguards of all persuasions, discharged Legionnaires (many of them not local), and many of the diverse nomad tribes supported the militarization and the subsequent comprehensive Army reforms. For a span, it seemed that the Lhotunic project had triumphed.

The cracks began to show even before the [[Second Great War]]. The High King died without issue, and his heir from the cadet branch of the family didn't quite share his views. Tracing his lineage from the ancient Phyllocid Dynasty, the new prince was obsessed with the restoration of the ancient glory of Yokuda. Forsaking the City of Sentinel, he moved his capital to Hegathe, and crowned himself not as a High King, but as a [[Sheklith]] - a title which is not quite easy to translate. A traditional translation renders it as ‘the Emperor’, but the source of that authority is spiritual, not civic.

The war itself shattered the illusion of peace completely. Forebear Imperial loyalists and former Legion soldiers urged joining in on the side of the Mede Empire, while both the Emperor and the Elden Yokeda bided their time, although for different reasons. The reasons of the conflict became ultimately moot, as the [[Silver Plague]] had devastated the region just as any other. The reconquest of the territories of the Old Kingdom of Hammerfell after the Plague ended had become known as the [[New Warrior Wave]].

The kingdom had never returned to its old borders, however. Not only had the traditionally Forebear territories of southern Iliac Bay resisted it in no uncertain terms, but many of the nomadic tribes of the desert had no desire to participate in the restoration project.

In fact, in that moment the traditional dichotomy that defined the understanding of the region from both the outside and the inside had revealed itself to be insufficient and, perhaps, plainly false. The narrative of the eternal confrontation between the 'tolerant and enlightened' (meaning absorbing Colovian culture) Forebears and 'barbarous and conservative' Crowns was long exploited first by the Remanian and then by the Septim colonial administrations. But without the traditional opponents to unite against, the Na-Totambu - what some still call the Crown - faction splintered into several contradictory interpretations of what makes a Redguard, especially when having to contend with the citizenship claims of the stranded Legion veterans and their families, and the familial connections stretching across the Iliac Bay to the north, Colovian Steppe to the west, and Abecean Sea to the south. Furthermore, that fracture resulted in three different civic and legal institutions that still coexist uneasily.

The royal palace in **[[docs/Wiki/Locations/Hegathe|Hegathe]]** - or Do-whacca-ding as it is named in Yoku - works hard on reconstructing the original faith of Na-Totambu, cleansing it from the taint of foreign worship. A new Temple of Satakal is led by an Archpriest, but considers the Sheklith its ultimate spiritual leader. It is the authority that takes upon itself all matters spiritual and religious - one among the latest innovations is the tax levied on 'false believers', and the licensing of magic use is also under the remit of this authority. Fortunately, the actual doctrines of the Temple are yet amorphous and non-exclusive enough so that most don't make an issue of swearing to one more god.

The other temples and faiths of the land do not follow the fundamentalist course quite so enthusiastically. First among them, the Temple of Tu'whacca-Arkay on **[[Stros M'kai]]** is famous among pilgrims. The other hybrid worships include Mara-Morwha, Z'en-Zeht and Kenarthi-Tava of the wandering caravans. Even the Temples of Sword-gods Diagna, Leki and Onsi, and the secretive individual Sword-god cults of the Army take on the characteristic features of both the traditional Nord hero-cults and Ebonarm worship of Freehold.

Where the royalists had concentrated on religion, the so-called 'young Yokuda' faction of the current Elden Yokeda had chosen to rally around the language. Yoku, preserved in recent centuries only in the nomadic areas and remote settlements, made a return. Not only had the whole military bureaucracy of the [[Stros M'kai]] headquarters switched to Yoku, but the practice of granting Yoku names and surnames had been used both as an award when given with titles and ranks to the former ra (soldiers), and as a punishment when military governors summarily listed the Yoku-styled names on the conscription census of their provinces.

What is curious from our understanding though is that beyond a religious tax and a military census, neither Do-whacca-ding nor Stros M'kai is performing what we are used to thinking of as the main purpose of government. There are no comprehensive import regulations, goods taxation, or trade agreements regulated by the state in a comprehensive way. Instead, the Totambu society is a patchwork of familial and clan obligations, gifts and mutual recognitions, which are governed and adjudicated case by case by the informal councils of the respected elders - mostly female ones, grandmothers, yok'shira.

Informal doesn’t mean lenient, however. A foreigner who tries to set up a stall without a family to vouch for him, and without preparing a gift for the local grandmothers, would find himself named tobr'a - useless, literally - and thrown out of the city on his ear, and his goods taken to recompense his rudeness. Thus, any trade should start by establishing the family and the lineage - do-tongo, sons and daughters, nieces and nephews, quite different from the Merish custom of tracing it to a singular heroic ancestor, yet somewhat similar. The propriety then demands a proper grateful gift (riglametha) to the elders, which is then going to be redistributed through the clan and the family as needed.[^1]

If Do-whacca-ding and Stros M'kai are the capitals of a Sheklith in his palace and an Elden Yokeda in his headquarters, **[[Gilane]]** is the third, softer one. The lineage songs, the banishment rites, the standing debts, all are routed through the singers of Gilane sooner or later. The elected representatives, the most honored yok'shira of their cities, convene yearly at the feast of Baranth Do, on the 18th of Evening Star, to resolve the long-standing issues that reach beyond their respective remits.

Of the other cities that may interest a traveler, [[Ska'vyn]] is the next important one. It was most densely settled by the Legion veterans from the eastern provinces, and as a consequence, a Potentate merchant is most likely to find a familiar relation there without paying, or in polite Redguard vernacular ‘gifting’, the outrageous adoption fees. 

[[Taneth]], traditionally another easy way in for a foreign merchant, now finds itself beset by the Freehold Republic. The cross-border relationships grew much more tense as a result, almost to a state of trade war. Claiming do-Shraj or do-Reymon lineage, while opening access to the market in principle, carries increasingly unfavorable gifting demands and barter rates.

[[Stonemoor]] is one of those cities that don’t quite fall under the three authorities at the same time. It is deep enough in the Colovian steppe that Yokeda’s troops would have to enter a full-scale war with the surrounding Bjoulsae nomads to claim it. And the local worship pays only lip service to Satakal’s cult, preferring the four-winged shelter of Kenarthi-Tava. But the familiar relationships between the local families, the [[Mother-Navigators]], and the wandering Legions remnants run deep enough that the trade routes of the whole region find it profitable enough to pass through this city.

[^1]: It makes it appear much more tame and fair than it is. But when square-jawed enforcers shake your ship purser down at the docks, an import tax, a protection fee and a gift for a kindly grandmother look remarkably similar.
