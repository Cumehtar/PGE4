To journey in the Kingdom of [[Greater Wrothgaria and Karth]] is to step back in time: here, in Tamriel’s north, is a land where one might be convinced that the Empire never fell. It is a simpler land, picturesque and scenic, a land of chapels and cathedrals with great stained-glass windows, a land where honor and duty, not coin, is the way of life for the countless thane-barons and commonfolk of the nation.

Let us begin our exploration of Wrothgaria in the east. Here, the Kingdom reaches south, to the foothills of the Jeralls themselves, to [[Falkreath]] Hold. Falkreath is honored in Wrothgaria as the birthplace of Empires - historically, the hold was the kingdom of Emperor Zero himself, Cuhlecain, whose conquests laid the foundation for his chosen successor, Tiber Septim, to unite Tamriel under his own banner. Centuries later, Falkreath would again become the foundation of the last bastion of the Septims - or so the Kingdom claims - when the Jarl and commander of the Imperial Garrison decided to march north together to secure the road to Solitude at the outset of the Silver Plague, establishing what is now known as the Legion Road and cementing the Queens of Solitude as the legitimate Imperial authority in the North. Falkreath’s southern position in the Kingdom has benefitted it greatly: trade through the Pale Passes the hold controls has made it rich, by the standards of the Kingdom, at least. Many in Wrothgaria are known to grumble that the men and women of Falkreath are beholden not to honor, but gold - a dangerous claim in this land of obligations. Others yet whisper that the Earl seeks to break away from Wrothgaria wholesale, joining the [[Colovian Estates]] - a claim not without merit, as visitors report that Earl Igor Skulnarson oft entertains magisters of [[Elinhir]] at his court.

Traveling north by the Legion Road brings one to perhaps the most idyllic of Tamriel’s landscapes: quaint villages with humble chapels, vast fields of lavender and flowers, hedged by snowberry bushes, and plots of the frost-resistant wheats and ryes favored by the Nords only rarely interspersed here and there. These alchemical ingredients are the main exports of the northern kingdom, and the landowners have grown wealthy from trade - hillforts renovated and grown resplendent, the Road itself wide and well-maintained, and even an occasional imported enchanted device. [[Rorikstead]], the Kingdom’s main - and indeed, true - city of the plains has risen far from its humble roots to become a power that rivals [[Whiterun]] to the east.

The Legion Road branches as it reaches the Hjaal River and the grain-estates of the Hailstone Valley. To the west, the Road takes us to [[Solitude]], the ancient capital of the Septims, while to the east, the Road plunges into [[Hjaalmarch Hold]], an administrative subdivision rendered largely powerless and insignificant by the Kingdom’s feudalism. Castle [[Snowhawk]] is the ostensible seat of power in the region, with its lords, the Oakenhearts, drawing levies and taxes from fisherfolk, grain-lords, and swamp-striders alike. The Oakenhearts have even entered into an alliance with Sload-Baron B’Kaaga of [[Drajmykr Marsh]], gaining access to magic and esoteric lore that their rivals in the region have no answer to, though the details - and price - of this deal are only whispered of.

The Legion Road’s eastern terminus is the town of [[Morthal]]. Famed as a source of rare and potent alchemical ingredients, Morthal is the home of the resurgent Ravencrone clan thanks to the efforts of Jarl Leofric Ravencrone, who may most often be found at court in Solitude. If one were to continue eastwards from Morthal - a challenge, to be sure, as there are scant few roads through the shifting morass of salt marsh - they would find themselves coming to the vaguely defined eastern borders of the Kingdom. Here there are few settlements, mostly mining colonies dependent upon centuries-old deals and debts for survival in the mountains and tundra.

Taking the western fork of the Road at the Hjaal will bring one to [[Haafingar Hold]], the ancient heartland of the Kingdom. Haafingar _(Sea-Spear Mountains, in the old Nordic tongue)_  
stretches from the barony of Mor Khazgur in the west to Solitude in the east, hugging the leeward face of the Kilkreath Mountains. Nigh-uncountable towns and villages march from the Karth River up the flanks of the mountains, most founded in the days of the Plague when Solitude’s population fled the urban center.

The settlement of [[Dragon Bridge]] is the only major crossing of the Karth River for most of the river’s length. This has granted the village great status and wealth in the Kingdom - indeed, lordship of the town has traditionally been held by members of the royal line, from the earliest days of the Kingdom. The current king, [[Gaspard Clovis]] Bjornsson Lariat-Septim de Haafingar II, hails from Dragon Bridge, having been raised to the throne at a young age after the untimely death of his aunt. Dragon Bridge boasts one of the largest cathedrals in the Nordic east after those of Solitude, holding shrines of particular grandeur dedicated to Stendarr, Zenithar, and Kynareth.

The city of Solitude itself is the final terminus of the Road and the ostensible seat of power in the Kingdom. Solitude’s harbor, seated at the mouth of the Karth River and Drajmykr Marsh, is the deepest berth for ships along the north coast, though in the absence of regular dredging for the past two centuries, has become noticeably silted in, limiting access to local, Nordic-style longships and shallower-keeled southern ships.

Departing from Solitude westward requires one to leave the beaten track and most maintained roads if by land, or brave the scarce-traveled sea lanes of the Sea of Ghosts if by ship. To do so is to delve even deeper into the past - westwards, the land is less tamed, the lords less prosperous, the wilds darker and deeper and stranger.

The coastal mainland west of Solitude and east of [[Far-rhun]] is sparsely inhabited. The shores are stone and gravel, pocked by tidepools and choked with driftwood and seaweed. Great forests of pine and spruce march up hills and cliffsides to the mountains of Wrothgar. In many places, the highest of fortifications are ancient and at times ill-maintained Imperial and Nordic forts, inhabited as in the east by local lords and ladies descended from Imperial garrisons. More often, though, construction is simply of dry-stacked stone, almost as in the Atmoran styles of old, nothing like the great modern earthworks common across the south. Here, the only thegn-barons are those in the Imperial forts - deeper in the hills and forests, it is common to find that the common folk call an Orc or Riekr chief their lord.

The town of [[Jehenna]] is the safest port of call and largest settlement along this stretch of coast between Solitude and Far-rhun - situated in a deep inlet, Jehenna is thus protected from the worst of the Sea’s fickle weather, though it can rarely be called warm. The evergreen forests surrounding the town are crowned with snow nearly year-round, only melting in the depths of summer, and the inlet itself is often choked with ice, unsuitable for deep-bottomed ships. Jehenna’s folk are of both Breton and Nord stock, so thoroughly mixed as to be impossible to tell where one ends and the other begins. Indeed, Jehenna’s rulers claim for themselves the title of _yeorl_ - an oddly Nordic affectation for what has traditionally been a part of High Rock, yet one perfectly at home in this land of longships and frigid waters.

Off the north coast, the island baronies in the Sea of Ghosts are among the most desolate in the Kingdom. Never known for their prosperity or fertility, these lands have become even less so, beaten by gales and blizzards from Atmora, beset by sea giant raids, and haunted by vampires lurking in ice floes. Towns and villages of the common folk have been abandoned for foreboding castles and fortresses, stone towers and drums that are equal parts harbor and communal home. Contact with these remote islands is intermittent even by the standards of Wrothgaria, with few brave enough to risk the journeys to and from with any regularity. Such isolation has set the region a culture and even a language apart from the mainland, with linguistic drift that far outstrips even the most remote mountain villages. Chapels here are few and far between, displaying little of the decoration, much less grandeur, seen elsewhere in the Kingdom, and it is not uncommon to find an island whose religion resembles more the Nordic faith of Snow-Throat than the orthodoxy of Solitude.

Far-rhun sits at the west end of the roads and sea-lanes connecting the Breton west to the Nordic east of the Kingdom. Cool in climate, Far-rhun suffered more than most Wrothgarian cities in the dual tragedies of Plague and weakened warm water currents. Years of poor - or even no - harvests, Imperial withdrawal, and sickness depopulated the city-state, and it has never fully recovered. Though superior to Jehenna in total population, the city’s urban population is small and centered around the port, while most of the common folk reside in a web of towns and hamlets dispersed around the borders of the city proper, each headed by their own mayor - _echevin_, in the local vernacular - home to a few extended families, and separated from the next by a mile or two of fields. Here, one travelling west will find the first of the Breton west’s _mage-echevins_ - mage-mayors, mage-lords, wizard-counts, and a host of other titles, each denoting one trained in the magical arts and ruling as aristocrat. While often compared to the historic Nibenese battlemage aristocracy or the mage-nobles of the Iliac League, these mage-echevins generally fall short of both in magical knowledge and political power alike. Indeed, the most magically inclined of these mage-lords are also the least politically powerful, instead content to remain in isolation in their towers, occasionally utilizing their skills and knowledge to aid the communities they ostensibly rule.

The Great Bay lies south and west of Far-rhun. Few Wrothgarian ships elect to traverse the mouth of the bay from Far-rhun to Northpoint, instead taking a circuitous route southwest and then north again. Southern ships with deeper keels, larger holds, and wealthier backers only rarely venture to the northern sea routes, and even more rarely visit the Bay - as a result, what foreign trade this region sees is from the Commonwealth or Resdayn’s north. Coinage of any sort is a rare sight, even for the highest lords, and what economy exists revolves around barter, duty, and obligation to a greater extreme than anywhere else in the Kingdom.

Travelling by road south from Far-rhun is nearly as difficult as the journey to Far-rhun along the north coast. Roads here often follow no direct route, instead forming a web between the villages of fishers, farmers, and herders that live between the waters of the Bay and the Wrothgarian mountains. Many of these roads shift from season to season, as floods and landslides destroy established routes and new ones are slowly made. The common folk of the region generally lack the manpower, expertise, material, and time to maintain major, well-engineered routes - most agriculture here is barely above a subsistence level, with most time given over to sowing and reaping crops of vegetables and buckwheat and moving their herds of small-framed cattle from pasture to pasture. This region boasts few major settlements, and few lords of note - by and large, the rustic nature of the area, the quaint chapels and priories, and the beautiful scenery are the only major draws.

The southernmost point of the Bay presents a challenge to travel, both by land and by sea. Several major rivers empty into the bay here, forming a morass of river deltas and floodplains, to say nothing of the maze of islands and rock crags off the coast. Safe passage across the mainland often requires one to negotiate with one of the Sload-Barons who have made the swamps their home, at strange, esoteric, and often obscene prices. By sea, the risk to one’s life is modestly lessened. Octagonal lighthouses staffed by priests of the Eight cling to spits of land scarcely large enough to hold the towers themselves, their beacons faithfully kept lit night and day. Here, it is often enough to hire locals as steersmen and navigators to hop between islands, paying in food, clothes, trinkets and faith.

The west of the Great Bay presents perhaps the greatest juxtaposition in the Kingdom. No thegn-barons possess grants to rule and protect the mountains of Wrothgar and King’s Guard, and the sorcerer-knights of the Iliac Bay are similarly uninterested in the backwater villages, leaving the inhabitants to fend for themselves. Here, in the forgotten corners, on the barely enforced borders where the light of the Eight does not reach, people defer to the cults of the [[Druids]] as the main religious, magical, and even political authority. Many of these mystics are itinerants, wandering from village to village to offer advice and blessings, while others yet live in remote groves as recluses. Despite this isolation - or perhaps because of it - travelers braving the journey to this region have reported that the mountains are unusually fertile in comparison to the lowlands, with notably more variety of crops.

Yet to the north, [[Shornhelm]] and [[Northpoint]] stand as bastions of civilization. The twin - or perhaps, triplet - capitals of the Kingdom, these baronies are the strongholds of the Breton west. This region, known historically as Rivenspire, may remind a traveler of the lands of Skyrim in the time of the Empire - a windswept land of moors and crags populated by hardy folk, above which flies the banner of a red dragon. Though nowhere near as prosperous as the Nordic east, Rivenspire is nonetheless the Kingdom’s second largest population center, being far more organized and centralized than their neighbors in Far-rhun. Shornhelm, seat of Lord Cynric Brunwulf-Lariat Mallory, presides over the hilly central region, where countless petty castles and hill-forts rule over their fiefdoms, often little more than a town, a valley, and the hills surrounding them. Lord Cynric himself is often absent from his holdings, instead styling himself as an adventurer - something that has not affected his baronies in the slightest.

Northpoint, the seat of Lord Cynric’s wife, thane, and heir apparent to the throne of the Kingdom, Jarl Fianna Wind-Speaker Lariat, is much the same as Shornhelm in organization if not terrain. Spanning the most northeastern coastline and land of Rivenspire, Northpoint’s gentler terrain of foggy hills, dales, and coves supports a larger population than neighboring Shornhelm. The city of Northpoint itself is the largest port in the western Kingdom, and the last (if one is sailing west) harbor capable of hosting deep-bottomed southern ships before reaching [[Daggerfall]], countless leagues to the southwest in the Iliac League. Most of the port’s traffic is thus local or ships from the Commonwealth - Northpoint has increasingly become a trade center for merchants and expeditions from Snow-Throat casting off to or returning from Atmora. Recent years have seen the import of small numbers of mechanical contraptions created by Commonwealth engineers intended for use in the fields of Rivenspire by Jarl Fianna, who, in the absence of her husband on his many voyages across the Kingdom and Tamriel’s North, has seen fit to take his responsibilities upon herself as well. Though obviously inferior to the soul-mechanisms of the Potentate, usage of these contraptions has allowed for a greater surplus of food to be produced by the people of Rivenspire - though small, enough for more consistent resupply of ships braving the northern sea-routes.
